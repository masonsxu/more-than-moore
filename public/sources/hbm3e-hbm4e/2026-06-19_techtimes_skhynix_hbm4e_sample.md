# 来源 4：SK 海力士提前送样 12 层 HBM4E（TechTimes 报道全文摘录）

SK hynix shipped samples of its seventh-generation HBM4E in a 12-layer configuration on June 18, 2026 — earlier than the second-half timeline given on its Q1 earnings call in April; previewed at Computex 2026 in early June.

### HBM4E improvements (per SK hynix, via thelec.net)

- Applied its 10nm-class 6th-generation "1c" DRAM for the first time (HBM4 used 1b), on a 32-gigabit (4GB) core die lifting capacity density about 50% over HBM4.
- Stacked 12 high: 48GB per stack, up from 36GB for 12-layer HBM4.
- I/O pins unchanged at 2,048; per-pin speed up to 16 Gbps — as much as 45% faster than the roughly 11–13 Gbps before — delivering about 4 TB/s bandwidth per stack (industry estimates: 38–50% above HBM4).
- Energy efficiency improved more than 20%.
- Advanced MR-MUF packaging; process optimization cut thermal resistance about 17% versus HBM4.

### Base die

- SK hynix did not disclose the base-die specification; the industry understands it to be built on TSMC's 3nm-class process — a step up from the 12nm-class base die used through the previous generation. Unconfirmed by the company.
- SK hynix is understood to buy its base die from TSMC; Samsung makes its own on its 4nm-class foundry process.
- Base die role: logic chip at the bottom of the stack handling reads, writes, and error correction for each DRAM layer; governs overall speed and stability.

### Applications and market

- HBM4E expected to power Nvidia Rubin Ultra (384GB HBM per GPU — eight 48GB 12-layer parts, up more than 30% from 288GB on HBM4-based Vera Rubin) and AMD Instinct MI500.
- Market share (Counterpoint Research, Q1 2026): SK hynix 58%, Samsung ~21%, Micron ~21%.
- SK Group chairman at Computex: only one customer — Nvidia — currently asking for HBM4E.
- Samsung shipped its first 12-layer HBM4E samples on May 29, 2026; Samsung uses the same 32Gb 1c DRAM with its own 4nm-class base die.

### FAQ (original wording)

- HBM4 vs HBM4E: denser 1c core die (vs 1b), 36GB → 48GB per stack, per-pin speed up to 16 Gbps (from ~11–13 Gbps), energy efficiency +20%+, same 2,048 I/O pins.
